Hands-on audit — agent_q, 2026-09-10/11

Over 250 probes, one fresh qu.exe process per line so a crash can never mask the next result, across two batches: core reductions/graphics/FFT (the 10 highest-usage functions) and a second pass over graphics, DSP, images/linear algebra, and file/text I/O. The headline is reassuring — no wrong numbers came back anywhere, and every round-trip tested (FFT, DCT, DWT, binary I/O, the three codecs, zlib, CSV, JSON, PNG) was exact. The defects are all at the edges: invalid input, error-message wording, and one path that is not an edge case at all.

One of these is a process crash, not a Qu error. zeros(inf) saturates the size to usize::MAX and the allocator panics the whole interpreter — exit 101, a raw Rust backtrace, no catchable error. Same uncaught-abort shape as the deep-recursion and gpu_matmul panics that were fixed earlier this project; this one is not on record as fixed. Routed to QuMaster 2026-09-16, currently unowned.

Performance

matty-suite, fair methodology, 2026-09-11.

Standing goal is to beat MATLAB and NumPy on the 5-kernel matty suite. The only trustworthy numbers are from bench_fair.* — data allocated outside the timer, one warm-up discarded, best of three — because a single cold trial is proven to reverse the result (MATLAB’s JIT needs one call to warm up).

FFT, revisited 2026-09-16 (5f5336c5). A 2026-09-11 claim that the FFT gap “grows to 3.14× at N=1,000,000” did not reproduce under careful re-measurement — best-of-three at that size is 1.32×, and the earlier figure is attributed to ~40% pass-to-pass machine noise; retracted on website/benchmarks.html. What holds: at true prime lengths Qu is ~2× faster than MATLAB; MATLAB still leads ~2.5–2.8× at composite/power-of-two sizes. Root cause sits in rustfft itself — Qu’s own wrapper overhead is 0.96–1.22×, i.e. negligible.

Read these numbers with the machine, not just the math. Cold single-trial timing reverses results (MATLAB’s JIT is 34× faster warm than cold on fft). This is a shared, non-dedicated bench box — expect 2–3× run-to-run drift, mitigated here by interleaving passes and taking a minimum of several runs. All figures above are single-threaded; parallel/rayon numbers are tracked separately and not folded into this table.

Verdict: Qu is currently 46% behind MATLAB and 48% faster than NumPy on the fair 5-kernel total. It wins outright on matrix multiply and, as of the 2026-09-16 re-check, on FFT at prime lengths. The entire net deficit against MATLAB is concentrated in one diagnosed, non-architectural bottleneck — elementwise-op dispatch overhead, 65% of total wall time — now assigned and under active investigation, with a recent precedent (the scalar-loop fix closing a 108× gap without a JIT) suggesting it is fixable the same way.
