Signal processing

Noise, in and out

putting noise in, and taking it out again.

catalog/qu_noise_and_removal.qu

The point of a noise module is not to make a signal look untidy. It is to let you state exactly how hard a problem is, hand the same problem to several methods, and see which one wins and where.

NOISE IS SPECIFIED IN DECIBELS. "Add noise of amplitude 0.1" is not a statement about a measurement -- it depends entirely on how big the signal is. "Add noise at 3 dB SNR" is, and it is what a datasheet, a standard and a reviewer all speak. Every kind here honours that number, including the four that are not linear in their own parameter and get there by solving for it.

NOISE IS NOT ONE THING. Using white noise for all of it is how a method comes to look robust in simulation and fail on the bench:

gaussian thermal, and the sum of many small independent causes pink 1/f flicker in semiconductors, sensor drift; worse the slower you measure, which is why a long integration is not free brown 1/f^2 a random walk -- baseline wander shot Poisson arrival of quanta; its variance is its mean, so it grows WITH the signal, unlike everything above quantize what the ADC does; correlated with the signal, which is why it sounds worse than its power suggests salt-pepper dead pixels, bit errors. Not additive: it REPLACES samples, which is exactly why a linear filter cannot remove it

INTERFERENCE IS NOT NOISE. Hum and EMI are deterministic -- they have structure, and that structure is what lets you remove them.

DISTORTION IS NEITHER. It is a function of the signal, so averaging does nothing at all and the only cure is not to distort.

What it exercises

  • add_noise(x, kind, snr=) — every kind, one parameter
  • hum / emf — mains and switching pickup
  • distort — clip, soft, crossover, harmonic
  • medfilt / smooth / savgol / hampel / detrend
  • measure_snr(clean, noisy) — the loop-closer

How it works

Ask for a number, get that number

print("Gaussian noise, asked for and measured:")
for target in (20, 10, 3, 0, -6)
    y = add_noise(clean, "gaussian", snr = target, seed = 7)
    print("  asked {pad_left(str(target), 3)} dB   measured {measure_snr(clean, y):.2f} dB")
end for
print("")

print("Every kind at 6 dB -- one parameter, one meaning:")
for kind in ("gaussian", "uniform", "pink", "brown", "blue", "shot", "quantize", "salt_pepper", "impulse")
    y = add_noise(clean, kind, snr = 6, seed = 3)
    print("  {pad_right(kind, 12)} {measure_snr(clean, y):.2f} dB")
end for
print("")

The pairing that matters: salt-and-pepper against a median

An average is pulled by an outlier in proportion to how far out it is. A median ignores it entirely, as long as fewer than half the window is corrupt. The gap below is twenty decibels.

sp = add_noise(clean, "salt_pepper", amplitude = 0.08, seed = 5)
sp_med = medfilt(sp, 5)
sp_avg = smooth(sp, 5, method = "moving")
print("Salt-and-pepper at 8% of samples:")
print("  as measured        {measure_snr(clean, sp):.2f} dB")
print("  median filter (5)  {measure_snr(clean, sp_med):.2f} dB")
print("  moving average (5) {measure_snr(clean, sp_avg):.2f} dB")
print("")

And the opposite case, so neither is mistaken for a universal answer

On plain Gaussian noise a moving average wins outright -- it is the optimal linear estimator for it. The median is for OUTLIERS, not for noise, and using one for the other costs you.

g = add_noise(clean, "gaussian", snr = 6, seed = 11)
g_avg = smooth(g, 11, method = "moving")
g_med = medfilt(g, 11)
print("Gaussian at 6 dB:")
print("  moving average (11) {measure_snr(clean, g_avg):.2f} dB")
print("  median filter (11)  {measure_snr(clean, g_med):.2f} dB")
print("")

Where Savitzky-Golay earns its place: when the SHAPE matters

A moving average is a low-pass filter and a peak is high-frequency, so it flattens the peak while it removes the noise. Savitzky-Golay fits a local polynomial instead, and keeps the height and width.

peak = zeros(N)
for i in 0 to N - 1
    u = (i - 1024) / 60.0
    peak[i] = exp(-u * u)
end for
noisy_peak = add_noise(peak, "gaussian", snr = 10, seed = 2)
sg = savgol(noisy_peak, 61, 3)
ma = smooth(noisy_peak, 61, method = "moving")
print("A Gaussian peak under 10 dB of noise, both smoothed with a 61-point window:")
print("  true height          1.000")
print("  Savitzky-Golay       {max(sg):.3f}")
print("  moving average       {max(ma):.3f}")
print("  (the average removes more noise and destroys the measurement)")
print("")

Interference, and why the harmonics matter

Mains pickup arrives through a symmetric nonlinearity -- a rectifier, a saturating core -- and a symmetric nonlinearity generates ODD harmonics. `harmonics = 3` therefore means 50, 150 and 250 Hz, and a notch at 50 alone leaves two thirds of the problem behind.

h = hum(clean, FS, freq = 50, harmonics = 3, snr = 6)
print("50 Hz mains hum with three odd harmonics: {measure_snr(clean, h):.2f} dB")
e = emf(clean, FS, carrier = 150, snr = 6)
print("EMI: a modulated carrier plus switching-edge bursts: {measure_snr(clean, e):.2f} dB")
print("")

Outliers are a different problem again

`hampel` replaces a sample by the local median ONLY when it is more than n_sigma robust deviations away. Everything it does not flag it leaves bit for bit -- which is the difference from a median filter, and the reason to reach for it when the data are good apart from a few spikes.

spiky = clean * 1.0
spiky[400] = 6.0
spiky[900] = -7.0
spiky[1500] = 5.5
r = hampel(spiky, 7, 3)
print("Three spikes in {N} samples:")
print("  hampel replaced {r.replaced} samples")
print("  error after      {measure_snr(clean, r.x):.2f} dB")
print("")

Baseline wander

drifting = clean + 2.0 + 0.0015 * (0 to N - 1)
flat = detrend(drifting, 1)
print("With a DC offset and a ramp: detrend recovers {measure_snr(clean, flat):.1f} dB")
print("")

Distortion is not noise

Averaging a hundred records removes noise and does absolutely nothing to distortion, because distortion is the same every time. A SINGLE tone, because THD counts every non-fundamental component as a harmonic -- and the two-tone signal above would have its second tone counted as distortion that is not there.

pure = sin(2 * pi * 31 * t)
print("Total harmonic distortion, in dB below the fundamental:")
print("  clean tone          {thd(pure):.1f} dB")
for kind in ("soft", "crossover", "harmonic", "clip")
    print("  {pad_right(kind, 12)} 0.4   {thd(distort(pure, kind, 0.4)):.1f} dB")
end for
print("  (averaging a hundred records changes none of these:")
print("   distortion is a function of the signal, so it is identical")
print("   in every record and averaging is powerless against it)")
d = distort(clean, "clip", 0.4)
