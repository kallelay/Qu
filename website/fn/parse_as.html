parse_as

Builtin index · Collections & strings

result = parse_as(s, template, [must_match=])

Input: s, template, must_match · Output: result

What it is

s and template are Strs. scanf backwards: "hello world 12".parse_as("hello world %d") gives 12. %d a whole number, %f any number, %s a run of non-space, %* anything. The template understands \t, \n, \r and \\ in its literal parts, so "%d\thello" and r"%d\thello" mean the same thing; a literal backslash is doubled.

Overloads

One call shape — this function is not overloaded.

How it works

parse_as(s, template) — scanf backwards, and the reason it exists is that a measurement file's header is a fixed sentence with numbers in it. "hello world 12".parse_as("hello world %d") gives 12. %d a whole number, %f any number, %s a run of non-space, %* anything at all. Literal text between the placeholders must match exactly, which is the point: a line that does not have the shape you said returns none rather than numbers pulled out of a different sentence.
