contourf

Builtin index · Plotting

contour(x, y, Z)

Input: x, y, Z · Output: no output

What it is

Iso-lines through scalar field Z (a Mat with len(y) rows and len(x) columns) over axis vectors x, y (Vecs of numbers); contourf fills the bands between levels instead of only outlining them. All three arguments are always required — contour(Z) alone is a runtime error (contour(x, y, Z) needs three arguments), even though Z alone is enough information for some other libraries' contour calls. levels= (integer count, or a Vec of explicit level values) controls how many iso-lines are drawn or exactly which.

Overloads

One call shape — this function is not overloaded.

How it works

contour(x, y, Z) / contourf(x, y, Z) -- iso-lines and filled bands over a scalar field. x and y are the axis vectors and Z is a matrix with y.len() rows and x.len() columns, matching what meshgrid implies and what matplotlib accepts. Passing the meshgrid matrices themselves works too: only their first row and first column carry new information, so they are reduced to axis vectors rather than rejected -- a script transcribed from Python passes contourf(KK, NN, Z) and should not have to be rewritten to say contourf(K, nuT, Z). NaN in Z masks a cell out, which is how a "the formula does not apply here" region is drawn (np.where(cond, v, np.nan) in the original).
