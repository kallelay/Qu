pptx.add_shape

Builtin index · File I/O

pptx.add_shape(p, i, kind, x=, y=, w=, h=, [fill=], [stroke=], [stroke_width=1.5], [dash=], [radius=], [text=], [size=], [bold=], [italic=], [color=], [font=], [align="center"], [name=])

Input: p, i, kind, x, y, w, h, fill, stroke, stroke_width, dash, radius, text, size, bold, italic, color, font, align, name · Output: no output

What it is

Adds a shape to slide i and returns its id. kind is "rect", "ellipse" ("oval") or "rounded_rect" -- placed by x, y, w, h -- or "line" / "arrow" (an arrowhead at the end), placed from (x=, y=) to (x2=, y2=) with only stroke=, stroke_width=, dash= and name= besides. Positions are millimetres from the top-left corner or lengths. fill= and stroke= take a colour name or #rrggbb, or "none"; the default is Qu's palette blue with no outline (an unfilled shape and every line get a dark outline, or they would be invisible). stroke_width is in points, dash is "solid", "dash", "dot" or "dashdot", radius (rounded rectangles) is the corner as a fraction of the shorter side, 0 to 0.5. text= puts text inside (newlines start paragraphs; size, bold, italic, color, font, align format it; the colour defaults to black or white, whichever reads on the fill). Colours are written explicitly rather than taken from the theme, so the shape looks the same in any deck. Nothing on the master, the layouts or the slide's animation timing is touched. *Added in v0.4.7*

Overloads

One call shape — this function is not overloaded.
