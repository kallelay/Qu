smith

Builtin index · Plotting

smith(Z, [z0=], [grid=], [color=], [lw=], [ms=])

Input: Z, z0, grid, color, lw, ms · Output: no output

What it is

Impedance on the reflection-coefficient plane: z = Z/z0, Γ = (z−1)/(z+1), drawn on the unit disc ruled by constant-resistance circles and constant-reactance arcs. Both families are exact — the Möbius map takes circles to circles. z0= defaults to 50 Ω; grid=false draws the locus alone. Sets equal aspect, because on unequal axes the circles become ellipses and every angle on the chart is wrong.

Overloads

One call shape — this function is not overloaded.

How it works

polarplot(theta, r) — plots (theta, r) polar-coordinate data by converting to Cartesian (x = r*cos(theta), y = r*sin(theta)) and drawing an ordinary line/scatter series on a square-aspect panel. Not a native polar-axis renderer — there are no circular gridlines, angular tick correctly-shaped polar curve today without a from-scratch renderer rewrite, at the cost of Cartesian-looking axes. smith(Z, [z0=], [rings=], [grid=]) -- impedance on the reflection-coefficient plane. z = Z / z0 (normalized impedance) G = (z - 1)/(z + 1) (reflection coefficient) The chart is the unit disc of G, ruled by two families of circles. Constant NORMALIZED RESISTANCE r maps to the circle centred at (r/(1+r), 0) with radius 1/(1+r); constant REACTANCE x maps to the circle centred at (1, 1/x) with radius 1/|x|, of which only the part inside the unit disc is physical. Both are exact, not approximations: the Moebius map that takes z to G takes circles to circles. Drawn as ordinary series, like the polar grid, so no second renderer is needed and every backend gets it for free.
